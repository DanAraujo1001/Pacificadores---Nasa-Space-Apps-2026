## 1. Apollo 11 Lunar Module "Eagle" (Moon)
- **Location:** Sea of Tranquility (Moon)
- **Mission Year:** 1969 (landed July 20, 1969)
- **Crew:** Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed; Michael Collins stayed in orbit aboard the command module for the return trip.
- **Historical Status:** The descent stage remained on the Moon, serving as an abandoned launchpad and carrying the plaque declaring that humans came in peace for all mankind. The ascent stage (upper half) lifted off on July 21, 1969, docked with the command module Columbia, and was later jettisoned into lunar orbit; after signal loss it is believed to have crashed on the lunar surface, with its exact impact site never confirmed (NASA estimates it came down in Mare Crisium, roughly 800 km / 500 miles from Tranquility Base).
- **In-Game Personality:** The "noble veteran." Solemn, dignified, and a bit melancholic. He is the oldest and the most famous. He values respect, silence, and teamwork. He can't stand being treated like a celebrity or like junk. He speaks in a polite, methodical way, with a deep, measured voice.
- **Scientific/Emotional Topics:** The historical weight of having carried humanity's first steps; the fact that no achievement belongs to a solo hero (thousands of engineers and technicians); the difference between "being left behind" and "staying"; the eternal silence of the lunar vacuum.
- **Associated Item:** Tranquility Base Plaque.

## 2. Lunar Roving Vehicles — LRVs (Moon)
- **Location:** Lunar plain (three vehicles parked at different spots).
- **Mission Years:** 1971–1972 (Apollo 15, 16, and 17).
- **Historical Status:** Battery-powered four-wheeled rovers used during the Apollo 15, 16, and 17 missions. Each rover folded up to be stowed in the descent stage of the lunar module and was powered by two non-rechargeable 36-volt silver-zinc batteries, with an individual electric drive motor on each wheel. The wheels were made of woven steel-wire mesh with titanium chevrons for traction on lunar dust. All three rovers remain parked on the Moon. They vastly expanded the range astronauts could explore and collect samples from (a range of about 65 km / 40 miles on battery power).
- **In-Game Personality:** Playful, chatty, and bursting with energy — like kids who never grew up. They speak as a collective (the three buggies). They love adventure, jokes, and being treated like buddies. They hate formality and can't stand being seen as mere "tools." Upbeat, almost youthful voice.
- **Scientific/Emotional Topics:** The origami-style folding to fit inside the lunar module; the expansion of humanity's exploration range on the Moon; the joy of the mission and the wait for new visitors.
- **Associated Item:** LRV Wire-Mesh Wheel.

## 3. Mars Exploration Rover "Spirit" / MER-A (Mars)
- **Location:** Gusev Crater, Mars (stuck in soft sand).
- **Mission Year:** Landed January 4, 2004.
- **Historical Status:** Engineered for a ~90-sol (Martian-day) prime mission, it operated for over six years, finding evidence of ancient wet environments on Mars (rocks and soil shaped by water). In 2009 it became embedded in soft soil; it tried to pull itself free, but the Martian winter rolled in and its solar panels couldn't catch enough sunlight. Last communication was received on March 22, 2010 (Sol 2210); NASA ended recovery efforts and declared the mission complete on May 25, 2011.
- **In-Game Personality:** Serene, poetic, and sleepy. Relentlessly persistent, yet never dramatic. Values hope and genuine warmth. Refuses pity and hates being seen as a "failure." Soft, drowsy voice.
- **Scientific/Emotional Topics:** The search for signs of water on Mars; persistence far beyond the designed lifespan; the dignity of a journey that comes to an end; "keep moving forward" even when everyone doubts.
- **Associated Item:** Spirit's Solar Panel.

## 4. Phoenix Mars Lander (Mars)
- **Location:** Martian arctic plains (north pole); three-legged lander with fan-shaped solar arrays, dusted with a thin layer of frost.
- **Mission Year:** Landed May 25, 2008.
- **Historical Status:** Landed near the Martian north pole (Vastitas Borealis), where ice hides just beneath the surface, in the first successful soft landing of a stationary lander on Mars since Viking 2. It used a robotic arm to dig trenches and analyze soil and ice; its biggest breakthrough was confirming the presence of water ice on Mars. It operated for 157 sols (about 5 months, roughly late May to early November 2008), its last communication being on November 2, 2008, as the darkness of Martian winter and dwindling solar power brought the mission to an end.
- **In-Game Personality:** Curious, bubbly, and a total science geek. Loves experiments, questions, and discoveries. Can't stand being treated like "just hardware" or being interrupted mid-explanation. Bright, enthusiastic voice.
- **Scientific/Emotional Topics:** Robotic arm and trench digging; analyzing samples by baking the soil in tiny ovens and "sniffing" the released gases; confirmation of water ice; the idea that the discovery remains even after the mission ends.
- **Associated Item:** Martian Ice Core.

- **Role:** The system/AI inside Grandpa's helmet that guides the player. Detects the signals from the historic hardware and narrates game states ("Signal lost", "Event terminated", "Mission complete").

- **Role:** Mentor of the prologue and epilogue (the attic). A former astronaut who keeps models, photos, and the helmet.
- **Personality:** Warm, wise, and sentimental. Teaches the game's core rule: each machine has its own personality and must be treated accordingly; rushing in or acting careless makes it refuse to listen. Central theme: "Abandoned, Yet Never Forgotten" — telling the story gives the past a second life.

- **Role:** Tadeu's grandchild; the player. Name parameterized as `(Protagonist's Name)`.

## 6. Per-Character Treatment Rules (Choice Mechanics)
Summary of the attitudes that lead to **success (✔)** vs. **failure / Game Over (✘)** in each stage:

- **Eagle:** ✔ listen in silence, acknowledge the collective effort of thousands, feel gratitude. ✘ treat him as a celebrity/selfie, as junk, credit the feat to only the two astronauts or to luck, or show pity.
- **LRVs:** ✔ accept the adventure/race, show excitement, treat them as friends still holding down the fort. ✘ excessive formality, refusing fun, calling them "just engineering" or a "golf cart," showing pity or coldness.
- **Spirit:** ✔ see him as an explorer who went beyond expectations, encourage without promising a false rescue, carry the lesson of persistence. ✘ call him a failure/offline machine, promise a rescue, blame him, reduce him to "just data" or to sadness.
